1 · Purpose & methodology

This capture documents a live operator-initiated chat exchange over the certificate-secured N2N federation (spec 060 claw-cert security, 063 wire hardening, H14 dialer-tier fix): a CML lab-health question sent from Nick's claw to both federated peers, recorded on the wire to verify that (a) all NCFED application traffic — including chat payloads — is TLS-encrypted, (b) the 063 endpoint-persistence machinery is live in both directions, and (c) failure modes surface as structured errors over the encrypted channel rather than as cleartext or silence. It is the third capture in the series (2026-07-14 cleartext era · 2026-07-17 first encrypted capture · this report).

Method. dumpcap ran for the full window on the any pseudo-interface with a three-port BPF filter; the chat was then initiated through the daemon's local HTTP API (POST /n2n/chat/send). Analysis used tshark/capinfos on a working copy; corroborating evidence was pulled from the daemon log (/tmp/bgp-daemon-v2.log), the federation database (~/.openclaw/n2n/federation.db), and the chat transcript store (~/.openclaw/n2n/chats/). All evidence blocks below are verbatim tool output.

7 · The only cleartext: BGP keepalives

Streams 2 and 3 are the mesh's BGP routing sessions: 82 KEEPALIVEs each over the window (≈30 s hold-timer cadence), plus ACKs. BGP here is the reachability substrate between claws; it carries no agent traffic, no NCFED JSON-RPC, and no chat content — the hex above is the entire recurring payload.

8 · Post-quantum note

No TLS handshakes occur in the window — both NCFED channels were established before capture start (John's at 13:04:33, per the daemon log), so no ClientHello/ServerHello and therefore no key-share group is present in this file. On this stack (both ends on OpenSSL 3.5-class hosts) the channels negotiate X25519MLKEM768; local posture intentionally reports pq_available=false until Python 3.15 exposes the negotiated group to the daemon (it will not claim what it cannot read). To put the PQ handshake on disk: start a capture, then bounce one channel (./scripts/peering-setup.sh start) and let the peer re-dial — the hello key-share extensions will show X25519MLKEM768.
